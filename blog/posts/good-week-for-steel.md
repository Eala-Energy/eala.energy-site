James sent me three headlines on Monday. Ampeak has installed every battery at its Newport site [[1]](https://nation.cymru/news/all-55-batteries-installed-at-new-energy-storage-facility/). Flower has filed permits for 2,000 MWh near Berlin [[3]](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/11/16/flower-plans-2-gwh-bess-in-germany-near-berlin/). Statkraft has switched on the first phase at Neilston [[4]](https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/energy-storage/statkraft-starts-commercial-operations-at-first-phase-of-750mw-neilston-bess-project).

“Good week for storage?”

Good week for steel. The figures tell a more complicated story.

Ampeak has a portfolio of roughly 2.6 GWh, but the 240 MWh at Newport is the part expected to start earning next year [[2]](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-11-12:newsml_RSL7953Ca:0-reg-ampeak-energy-ltd-company-update-and-bond-update/). Neilston has consent for 750 MW, of which 50 MW is now operating after more than three years of construction [[4]](https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/energy-storage/statkraft-starts-commercial-operations-at-first-phase-of-750mw-neilston-bess-project). Flower’s 2 GWh is scheduled for 2029 [[3]](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/11/16/flower-plans-2-gwh-bess-in-germany-near-berlin/).

“That’s how large projects are phased.”

True, and the financing tells you why. Newport’s first phase was funded against a development plan. Ampeak intends to finance the expansion to 480 MWh once that first phase is operating [[2]](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-11-12:newsml_RSL7953Ca:0-reg-ampeak-energy-ltd-company-update-and-bond-update/). By then, lenders will have something more useful than a model: a battery that has connected, cycled and generated revenue.

There is a less comfortable detail in Ampeak’s story. This month it asked bondholders to defer £12.7m of repayments for another two years, at 13% interest, because NESO has yet to confirm connection dates for the rest of its pipeline [[2]](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-11-12:newsml_RSL7953Ca:0-reg-ampeak-energy-ltd-company-update-and-bond-update/). Ampeak argues that connection reform has changed when the value can be realised, rather than the value itself. That distinction matters to investors, although it does little for near-term cash flow.

“So what would you do differently?”

I’d look at smaller, repeatable projects. Put a few megawatts of containerised compute alongside six to eight hours of storage at a wind site where generation is regularly curtailed because the transmission network is congested. Some of that electricity could be consumed locally rather than exported through an already constrained connection.

Build one, establish what it costs to operate and what it actually earns, then replicate it. By the time you’re financing site ten, you have operating data from nine others. Batteries and GPUs can be procured in tranches rather than committing to an entire pipeline at today’s prices.

That flexibility has value. In DLA Piper’s survey of 550 energy storage participants, 23% reported viable projects being deferred or cancelled because of critical-mineral price volatility [[5]](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/critical-minerals-price-swings-stall-energy-storage-projects).

“Small sites will cost more per megawatt-hour.”

Almost certainly. A 500 MW battery near Berlin should enjoy substantial economies of scale. Britain needs large batteries too, particularly as more wind capacity comes online and increases the need for flexibility. The trouble is that a battery due in 2029 cannot help manage today’s constraints.

There is room for both approaches. My preference would be to establish a modular design, get it earning, and use each operating site to reduce the financing risk of the next. The higher unit cost may be a reasonable price for shorter development cycles and less capital tied up waiting for a connection date.

I’d be interested in how lenders would view that proposition. Would three operating sites be enough to finance the next batch, or would a credit committee want to see ten?
